People use "pressure washing" to mean any exterior cleaning, but there are really two different methods, and using the wrong one is how homes get damaged.

## Pressure washing

High pressure water, typically 2,500 to 4,000 PSI. Great for concrete, brick pavers, and other hard surfaces. Terrible for siding, painted wood, shingles, and anything with seams, because it can strip paint, force water behind walls, and etch soft materials.

## Soft washing

Low pressure (under 500 PSI, close to a garden hose) combined with a cleaning solution that kills algae, mold, and mildew. The chemistry does the work. It's the right method for siding, stucco, roofs, fences, and painted surfaces.

## The quick guide

| Surface | Method |
| --- | --- |
| Vinyl or Hardie siding | Soft wash |
| Brick walls | Soft wash |
| Stucco | Soft wash |
| Asphalt shingle roof | Soft wash, no pressure |
| Concrete driveway | Pressure wash |
| Wood deck | Low pressure plus wood cleaner |

If a company quotes you a house wash and shows up with only a pressure washer and no chemical injection, ask questions before they start.
